Diffuse Violence

2025-06-17 Comments

Violence is a mindset that is practiced and reinforced. Whether violence is practiced at home or abroad, it normalizes and invites violence as a prescribed solution.

We live in an attention economy. Rather than a world that advertises bitcoin and bombs; society has an opportunity to focus on creative diplomacy and innovation. Each stick that is swung moves more minds towards the conclusion that sticks -- or maybe rocks -- are the necessary response.

Eventually, this mindset will become manifest. At this moment, an existential fuse will be lit. Society will be so submerged in back-and-forth violence that it will be internalized: the situation is existential and de-escalation will only be achieved after one side has *arrived* at a position of clear superiority. At which point, the defeated population may be eradicated, systematically isolated, made subservient, etc, such that a future threat is effectively "neutralized".

I am in no position of prescience to claim this pattern is or isn't inevitable; just to cite its prevalence in the past. This pattern has played out hundreds upon hundreds of times throughout history at different scales. Through it all, civilians have been systematically starved by both sides; deported as refugees; sent to ghettos, internment camps, killing fields, concentration camps; irradiated by atomic bombs; napalmed.

In my opinion, eliminating war seems an unlikely outcome in a world of 8B people with different needs, circumstances, and beliefs, who are all trying to survive and thrive in a world of limited and differently-distributed resources. But society does have some collective leverage to push for strategies that either escalate us towards or away from violent conflict.

At a high level, for a specific issue, we might try to:

  1. Find productive ways to lessen the pain and hardship others are experiencing, in a way that builds good will and diffuses animosity (no easy feat when even the the core notion of equality v. equitability is a hot button)
  2. We exacerbate this pain and hardship to draw clear lines and activate collective action.

We live in an interesting and unprecedented time where the world's people are somewhat united globally by instant access to the Internet and international news. This has the potential to amplify, dampen, or spread news of conflict at a fast clip. The circumstances may also present a unique challenge: an ecosystem laden with so many hot topics to simultaneously debate, that it can become difficult to reach critical mass or consensus to effectively addressing any of them.

Of the strategies above, I really hope we can shift our efforts globally towards de-escalation, repair, and good will where possible. I also understand it's not always possible. I hope the world's leaders will consider these options carefully and prioritize the safety and well being of the world's citizens -- not just those within their walls.
